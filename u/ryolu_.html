  9. 10.25Oct 25周日Sun查看本期View issue

    Ryo Lu 发了一篇长文，回顾自己靠反复试错学会编程与设计，担心当机器直接给出可用结果，人们会跳过理解过程、错过本该学会提出的问题。他也担心 agent 永远耐心在线，会让人更少去面对真实的人与人之间的摩擦，而很多友谊和协作恰恰从那里开始。他想要的不是没有挣扎的生活，而是有人一起挣扎。

    Ryo Lu wrote a long reflection on learning through struggle, recalling how he learned programming and design by trying, failing, and starting over until it "became like water." He worries that asking a machine and moving on lets results look complete while we miss the questions we never learned to ask, and that agents, always patient and ready, make it easier to avoid the discomfort of working with people. He pictured each of us with our own agents and little worlds running on server farms owned by companies, asking whether that is freedom or "a comfortable kind of confinement." His conclusion: "I don't want a life without struggle. I want people to struggle with," like a jazz band where nobody knows where they'll end up.

  11. 10.6Oct 6周二Tue查看本期View issue

    Ryo Lu 写下一篇纪念 Steve Jobs 的长文：从米色机箱到 iMac G4，他因为买不起 Mac 就把自己的 PC 改成 Mac 的样子，改图标字体窗口，学做那种闪亮按钮，由此进入界面设计。他刻录 keynotes 反复研究，第一台 iPod 是黑色 nano。他觉得 Steve 走后行业陷入循环：不断精修、更好的供应链、更多让人刷下去的方式，才华花在微小改进上，真正伟大而用心的东西空间很小。他怀念现场 demo 里有人在台上玩自己参与做的东西，那种兴奋、玩笑和出错都很有人味。他还发布了 ryOS Subtitles 的 Chrome 扩展，可以看 Netflix 时同时显示任意两种语言字幕，并为日语、中文、韩语提供发音指南。

    Ryo Lu, who designed Cursor, Notion, and Stripe, wrote a long reflection on Steve Jobs and the state of product design, lamenting that since Jobs died we've been stuck in a loop of constant refinement, better supply chains, and more ways to keep people scrolling. He misses live demos and the human feel of keynotes, and wishes we spent more time learning from people's lives rather than trying to beat humans. He also shipped ryOS Subtitles for Chrome, letting language learners watch Netflix with subtitles in any two languages plus pronunciation guides for Japanese, Chinese, and Korean.

  13. 10.3Oct 3周六Sat查看本期View issue

    Ryo Lu 发了一篇长文，警告"向均值收敛"：当所有人看别人做什么来决定自己做什么，最终什么都做不出来，只剩下一波平庸之作。他认为 AI 让这个循环变得毫无摩擦，但接触所有视角不等于拥有自己的视角，呼吁像艺术家一样做产品。

    Ryo Lu published a long essay on convergence to the mean, arguing that when everyone looks at everyone else to decide what to make, we eventually stop making anything of our own. He warned that AI makes this loop frictionless, producing a tidal wave of mid. His prescription: approach making things more like artists, developing a perspective through the work rather than borrowing answers from a competitor's roadmap.
