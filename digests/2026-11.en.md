## 🎙 PODCASTS
The takeaway: Arca treats AI as the machinery behind the advisor, not the advisor itself, and insists on owning its own harness rather than renting a lab's managed agents.

On Latent Space at the AI Engineer New York conference, Aman Singh, who leads the AI platform team at Arca, walks through how the AI-native wealth management firm, which recently raised sixty-four million, runs long-horizon financial work with agents. Clients never interface with AI directly; they experience service delivered through human advisors while agents handle the back office. A tax bill case can span ten days: agents analyze where a client's money sits and the optimal way to liquidate to produce a hundred thousand dollars, but strict guardrails keep humans in the loop for money movement, liquidation, and client communication, with a deep custodian integration providing the execution APIs.

Model choice runs on internal benchmarks, not external ones. Singh notes that despite strong external claims for **Luna**, it was too slow and costly for Arca, so the firm stays model-agnostic with its own harness, tiering heavier models like **Opus**, **Sonnet**, or heavy **GPT** models for complex tasks such as tax analysis and lighter models for background work. Long-horizon continuity comes from per-client memory systems plus a nightly "dreaming" reflection process, so a fresh signal like a phone call can be matched to an ongoing tax bill. Safety is deterministic at the edges: every database write gets an LLM-based verification, prompt-injection checks run on inbound emails, documents, and calls, and outbound data passes PII checks plus a consensus of small models against exfiltration. Auditability is by default, with a change log for every agent and human action.

On the central philosophy, Singh says: "Agents prompt people more than people prompt agents." Agents pull in the right actor at step seven only when a signal demands it. He is bullish on frontier models but wary of managed agents, since letting a provider own memory and operational knowledge means handing off control of your intelligence. Feedback loops turn every staff edit or cancellation, paired with its agent trace, into evals, and a nightly meta agent suggests harness changes, though a human engineer still reviews prompt changes because agents tend to bloat prompts with every fix. Knowledge lives as files curated by experts in a Knowledge tab, and verifiers catch stale facts like prior-year tax brackets or non-compliant promises of returns. One prospect learned from agent analysis that their existing advisor charged hidden fees and was not even fiduciary.
